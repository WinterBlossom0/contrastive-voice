## 2. Flaw by flaw

### 2.1 Volume and projection

**Reasons found.** (R1) low vocal effort (soft voice, no adaptation to distance or room); (R2) the recording chain (distance, gain, AGC, compression) - not a speaker property; (R3) declination toward phrase end and breath support (section 2.2). No source was found that treats "uneven loudness" as a flaw with its own pattern.

**Pattern of R1 - effort changes more than level.**
- [lienard1999] A (abstract): three efforts elicited by distance 0.4 / 1.5 / 6 m, ten speakers, isolated French vowels. "F0 and F1 were highly correlated with vocal effort and varied at rates close to 5 Hz/dB for F0 and 3.5 Hz/dB for F1"; "when the overall amplitude is increased by 10 dB, A1, A2, and A3 are increased by 11, 12.4, and 13 dB", "revealing a change in spectral tilt".
- [traunmuller2000] A: distances 0.3-187.5 m, men, women, children, one sentence. "In addition to levels and emphasis, vowel duration, F0, and F1 were substantially affected."
- [sundberg2006] A: 15 male and 16 female readers of a text at several loudness degrees; alpha = "the ratio of spectrum intensity above and below 1000 Hz"; "mean range of equivalent sound level (L(eq)) amounted to about 28 dB and the mean range of alpha to 19.0 and 11.7 dB for the female and male subjects"; alpha predictable from Leq with mean absolute errors of 0.1-0.7 dB. *Derived:* about 0.7 (women) and 0.4 (men) dB of alpha per dB of level, so a 6 dB effort drop moves alpha by roughly 2.5-4 dB.
- [cooke_lu2010] F (abstract; Sec. III): in noise "speech output level and fundamental frequency increased and spectral tilt flattened in proportion to the energetic masking capacity of the background"; tilt = "linear regression of the long-term average spectrum (0-8 kHz), expressed in dB/octave". [lu_cooke2009] F: the flattening of tilt, not the F0 rise, "contributed greatly to the intelligibility gain". [summers1988] A: Lombard speech also differs in "formant frequencies and short-term spectra of vowels".
- Not uniform across measures: [zhang_hansen2007] F (Table 1) reports LTAS regression slopes of -6.71 (soft), -8.29 (neutral), -8.27 (loud), -7.51 (shouted) dB/octave, i.e. no monotonic change, on 12 speakers. [harwardt2011] F (Tables 1, 3): tilt and centre of gravity change strongly for vowels and sonorants (vowel centre of gravity 916 -> 1204 Hz normal -> loud), obstruents are "less influenced by vocal effort"; a narrow-band energy ratio (2800-3000 Hz over 450-600 Hz) was the worst discriminator. The measure definition matters.
- Effort is not only level for the listener either. [eriksson2002] A / [eriksson1999] F: "Increased vocal effort also involves the emphasis of higher frequency components and increases in F0 and F1. This should allow listeners to distinguish it from decreased distance, which does not have these additional effects. It is shown that listeners succeed in doing so on the basis of single vowels if phonated, but not if whispered". [tjaden2014] F (abstract): 78 speakers, "Sentences were equated for peak amplitude ... Loud and clear but not slow conditions improved intelligibility". [graetzer2017] F (abstract): 19 speakers, normal and loud; signals "amplitude-normalised"; "Spectrum balance was observed to predict LD" (listening difficulty).

**Pattern of R2 - recording chain.** Level changes; spectral balance, F0 and F1 do not (the contrast drawn in [eriksson1999]). A pure gain edit is this pattern, not R1 (the earlier research said the same: `features/REPORT.md` sec. 8 item 4).

**Methods for the R1 pattern.**
- *Alpha ratio / spectrum balance.* Definition above [sundberg2006]. Already defined in the project (1000-5000 Hz over 50-1000 Hz, voiced frames). Validated as a loudness correlate within a voice; no threshold for "too soft" is published. Confounds: vowel identity, channel equalisation, sex (range 19.0 against 11.7 dB).
- *Spectral emphasis.* [eriksson2001] F (Sec. 2): "L0 was defined as the level of the signal after low-pass filtering at 1.5 F0 (-3 dB), with continuous adjustment of the cut-off frequency of a 4th order Butterworth filter. Emphasis was defined as L - L0"; it "is not affected by variation in F0 as such, but it is affected by between-vowel variation". [heldner2001] F (Sec. 1-2): same measure with the utterance-mean F0, rationale "to separate the fundamental from the rest of the harmonics ... and to obtain a normalized measure of the energy in the higher frequency bands"; duration and F0 are the features "relatively insensitive to channel effects such as distance from the speaker to the microphone". Being a difference of two levels of the same frame, the measure cancels gain.
- *Low/high spectral ratio, HNR, SPL as predictors of rated effort.* [mckenna2018] A: 26 speakers, /ifi/; "mean SPL, L/H ratio, and HNR" predicted speaker and listener ratings; mean F0 predicted listener ratings only.
- *Absolute effort class without level.* [kodali2023] F (Sec. 3, Tables 1-2): 50 speakers, four classes (soft, normal, loud, very loud), every signal divided by its maximum so that "the original intensity information ... is intentionally removed"; best conventional feature (mel-spectrogram) 64 +/- 2 %, wav2vec 2.0 layer features +5 % absolute. [omidi2026] F: same corpus, non-calibrated, fine-tuned WavLM-Base 78.2 %. Needs labelled training data; errors fall between adjacent classes.
- *Absolute level norms exist but cannot be applied to a file.* [rindel2019] F, Table 2 (S for Lazarus / ISO 9921): A-weighted level at 1 m: 48 relaxed, 54 relaxed/normal, 60 normal/raised, 66 raised, 72 loud, 78 very loud. [hunter2021] F: speakers changed level "approximately 5 dB between the elicited effort level goals". [zhang_hansen2007] F: "it may not be possible to know the exact dB-SPL level since recording levels and an AGC may be used".

**Fit.** Level per frame exists; alpha exists as a display value; F0 exists. Spectral emphasis needs one extra filter per frame driven by the existing F0 track (no model). Judged against: the speaker's own median (cancels a fixed channel and the speaker's habitual tilt) and against the reference reading of the same words (cancels vowel identity). *Inference:* a local fall of level together with a fall of alpha and F0 is the effort pattern; a fall of level alone is the recording pattern; broadband compression changes level dynamics but not per-frame spectral balance, so an alpha-based flag would not share the known compression false positives - not tested in any source read. Controlled edit: gain fade plus a spectral tilt filter (alpha lowered by about 0.4-0.7 dB per dB of fade, *derived* from [sundberg2006]) plus a small F0 lowering; time labels stay exact. The edit does not reproduce the change of glottal source that causes the tilt.

### 2.2 Trailing off, breath support

**Reasons found.** (R1) ordinary declination: subglottal pressure and source strength fall through the utterance; (R2) the phonation-ending gesture (glottis opens, pressure drops); (R3) low lung volume / running out of air; (R4) phrase-final creak as a habit. R1 and R4 occur in good speakers too, so the flaw is the amount, not the presence.

**Patterns.**
- R1: [pierrehumbert1979] A: "the amplitude downdrift which normally accompanies fundamental frequency declination may have an important role in the perception of phrasing". [nichasaide2015] F (Sec. 3.1, Table 1): short Irish sentences, inverse-filtered source; excitation strength EE declines at -7.3 dB/s in declaratives (R2 = 0.49), -7.8 and -9.5 dB/s in questions; "a gradual decrease in the level of the source excitation and an increase in the relative dominance of the lower end of the source spectrum, as would be indicative of an increasingly lax mode of phonation"; the authors call the data "limited". [yuan_liberman2014] F, S for Strik & Boves 1995: "it is the global downtrend of the subglottal pressure that determines the global downtrend of F0".
- R2: [slifka2006] A: "Between the start of the final fall in signal amplitude and the end of phonation, the alveolar pressure falls by 1.2-2.6 cm H2O, on average, and glottal area increases by a factor of 4.0 (median)"; the same gesture ends utterances with regular and with irregular phonation. [keating2015] F (Sec. 3-4): this "nonconstricted creak" is "attested utterance-finally, with the vocal folds beginning to spread before the utterance is over"; "Low HNR values indicate less strong periodic excitation relative to glottal noise".
- R3: [watson2003] A: eight women starting speech at low, typical, high lung volume: "with increasing lung volume initiation levels, average sound pressure level, average fundamental frequency, and declination rate of fundamental frequency increased ... vowel space was significantly smaller during low lung volume initiation". [winkworth1994] A: in reading, inspirations "occurred almost exclusively at grammatically appropriate places"; lung volumes were larger for "louder utterances" and "longer utterances". [vanpuyvelde2018] F (Table 2, as extracted): physical load leads to "inappropriate breathing pause placements".
- R4: [wolk2012] A: 34 female students; "approximately two-thirds of this population used vocal fry and that it was most likely to occur at the end of sentences". [drugman2014] F: creak is heard when F0 falls "below some auditory threshold in the vicinity of 70 Hz"; "considerable speaker-dependent variability".

**Methods.**
- *Intensity decay slope.* [rosen2005] A: "decline in vocal intensity (determined by linear regression of the intensity envelope)" compared across vowel prolongation, syllable repetition, sentences and conversation; in Parkinson's disease "intensity decay is not a robust symptom" and slopes in conversation "were more variable". No norm for healthy read speech was found. The project already defines the same quantity (phrase energy slope, dB/s) in `features/REPORT.md` 1.2 without using it.
- *Creak / irregular phonation.* Dedicated detectors use trained classifiers on features such as H2-H1 and residual peak prominence [drugman2014] F. Untrained proxies named by the sources: very low F0 (< ~70 Hz), irregular F0, low HNR [keating2015].
- *Breath-group structure.* Pauses at non-boundaries and short groups [winkworth1994; vanpuyvelde2018].

**Fit.** Per pause-delimited phrase: least-squares slope of voiced-frame level; last-word level minus phrase median; fraction of the last word's vowel frames that are unvoiced or below the F0 floor. All from existing frames and word timing; HNR would need one Praat call. Because R1 and R4 are normal, the only defensible judgement is against the reference reading's own value for the same phrase and against the speaker's other phrases; no absolute norm exists. Controlled edit: a gain ramp over the last words (the present fade when placed at a sentence end) with a matching tilt ramp; irregular phonation cannot be imitated by the present editors except by lowering F0.

### 2.3 Voice quality under nervousness

**Reasons found.** Stress arousal (respiration, laryngeal tension); cognitive load; clinical tremor and dysphonia (different populations).

**Patterns, and how reliable they are.**
- Reviews agree only on F0, and not firmly. [giddens2013] A (>50 studies): "Increase in fundamental frequency is the most commonly reported effect of stress in well-controlled trials. The trend, however, is not universal. A reduction in noise as reflected by the diminished vocal jitter is reported, but less frequently." [veiga2025] F (abstract): pooled F0 increase SMD = 0.55 (95 % CI 0.30-0.80, I2 = 69 %); "trim-and-fill attenuated the pooled estimate to a nonsignificant effect (SMD = 0.1710)"; "nonsignificant results for men and standardized speech". [schewski2025] F (abstract): "Stress was associated with increased F0 and intensity"; "No consistent acoustic patterns were identified for fear or anxiety." [kirchhubel2011] A: "inter-speaker variability must not be overlooked".
- Perturbation measures disagree in direction. [mendoza1998] A: stress gave "an increase in jitter and shimmer". [wang2025] F: "a robust negative relationship between jitter and state anxiety"; jitter "has demonstrated mixed results". [albuquerque2021] F: "Loudness and harmonic-to-noise ratio (HNR) ... have an irregular performance ... either no change, decrease or increase".
- Perturbation measures follow loudness. [brockmann2008] A: 57 adults, "Jitter and shimmer significantly increased with decreasing voice loudness, especially in phonations below 75 dB and 80 dB". [brockmann2011] A: "voice SPL has the single biggest impact on jitter and shimmer". [hemanackah2003] A: "Acoustic measures of jitter, shimmer, and noise-to-harmonic ratio (NHR) are often unreliable"; CPP on running speech is "more reliable".
- Tremor. [hollien1987] A: with commercial "voice stress" analysers built on laryngeal microtremor, "correct stress/nonstress identifications occurred only at chance levels". [anand2012] A (essential tremor, synthetic variation): rate 3-12 Hz, depth 2-32 Hz; "Perceived severity of tremor increased with" both, depth "acting as the primary acoustic cue". No study was found that measures tremor in nervous public speakers.
- Public speaking specifically: Goberman et al. 2011 and Laukka et al. 2008 could not be opened; through [albuquerque2021] only: F0 variability "evidenced to be a good indicator of anxiety symptoms, according to Hagenaars and Minnen and Goberman et al."; [hagenaars2005] A: "Pitch variability was lower in Fearful than in Happy speech".

**Methods.**
- *Tremor indices.* [bruckl2012] F: frequency (FTrF/ATrF) and intensity (FTrI/ATrI, % of mean) of "the strongest low-frequency modulation" of F0 or amplitude, from autocorrelation of the de-trended contours with a threshold of 0.15; "The expected input is a sound file that captures a sustained phonation". Not defined for running speech.
- *Cepstral peak prominence.* [hillenbrand1994, hillenbrand1996] A: periodicity measures explain about 80 % (vowels) and 70-85 % (sentences) of variance in rated breathiness. [murton2020] A: cut-offs 9.33 dB (Praat) and 6.11 dB (ADSV) on the Rainbow Passage; below them "the presence of a voice disorder with up to 94.5% accuracy" (295 patients, 50 controls). This is a published absolute norm, for disorder, not for nervousness.
- *Jitter, shimmer:* published pathology thresholds exist ([vanpuyvelde2018] F quotes 1.040 % and 3.810 %), for sustained vowels at controlled loudness.

**Fit.** F0 is available, but a raised mean F0 can only be judged against the same speaker's calm baseline, which a single upload does not contain; the reference is another speaker. Voice quality is a speaker trait first, so reference-relative comparison is not meaningful. The only within-clip quantity the sources support is change over time (e.g. the phrase-final loss of periodicity in 2.2). The earlier decision to exclude HNR, CPP, jitter and shimmer from scoring is consistent with these sources. Controlled edit: sinusoidal F0 modulation (3-12 Hz, stated depth) would imitate clinical tremor exactly; nothing read shows that nervous speakers produce it.

### 2.4 Clarity and articulation

**Reasons found.** (R1) hypo-articulation - the talker economises effort (H&H account, through [smiljanic2009] F: "the trade-off between clarity of speech (listener-oriented output) and economy of effort (talker-oriented output) (H&H Theory, Lindblom 1990)"); (R2) undershoot caused by speaking fast; (R3) casual reductions and deletions; (R4) low vocal effort or low lung volume; (R5) motor impairment (dysarthria) and (R6) second-language pronunciation - other populations with the same surface flaw and the best-developed measures; (R7) the channel.

**Patterns.**
- R1, the clear-versus-conversational bundle. [picheny1986] A: "In conversational speech, vowels are modified or reduced, and word-final stop bursts are often not released. In clear speech ... stop bursts, as well as essentially all word-final consonants, are released"; "the RMS intensities for obstruent sounds, particularly stop consonants, is greater in clear speech"; "changes in the long-term spectrum are small". [smiljanic2009] F: clear speech has "a decrease in speaking rate (longer segments as well as longer and more frequent pauses), wider dynamic pitch range, greater sound-pressure levels, more salient stop releases, greater rms intensity of the non-silent portions ... of obstruent consonants, increased energy in 1000-3000 Hz range of long-term spectra"; "One feature that has consistently been shown to accompany naturally produced English clear speech is vowel space expansion". Size of the listener effect: 17 percentage points [picheny1985] A; 3-4 dB of speech reception threshold [liu2004] A.
- R1 without slowing down. [krause2004] A: clear speech at normal rate differs by "increased energy in the 1000-3000-Hz range of long-term spectra and increased modulation depth of low frequency modulations of the intensity envelope", plus stop-burst frequency, VOT and short-term vowel spectra; [smiljanic2009]: at normal rate "no significant vowel space expansion", so "vowel space expansion is not a necessary clear speech feature".
- R2. [lindblom1963] A: formants reach their targets as "an exponential function" of vowel duration. [moon1994] A: "formant patterns were systematically displaced in the direction of the frequencies of the consonants of the adjacent ... context; ... those displacements depended in a lawful manner on vowel duration; ... this context and duration dependence was more limited for clear than for citation-form speech"; clear samples "were not merely louder". [turner1995] A: vowel space area changes with habitual / fast / slow reading.
- R3. [ernestus_warner2011] F (Sec. 2): variation "is far more extensive in spontaneous conversations than in formal or read speech"; reporting Johnson (S): "6% of the content word tokens and 4.5% of the function word tokens were produced with at least one syllable less", "5-12% of the word tokens lacked at least one segment". [warner2011] A: "greater reduction in connected and spontaneous speech ... approximant-like tokens even in careful speech". [yun2020] F: flaps occur with closure and release, closure only, or as "a shallow valley in the waveform amplitude". [aylett2004] A: an "inverse relationship between language redundancy and duration".
- R4. [watson2003] A (smaller vowel space at low lung volume). [tjaden2004] A: in reading, "Vowel acoustic distinctiveness, as indexed by vowel space area, was maximized in the slow condition, but stop consonant acoustic distinctiveness, as indexed by first moment difference measures, was maximized in the loud condition".
- Talkers differ in which cues they use: [ferguson2007] A, [maniwa2009] A ("talkers differed widely in the types and magnitude of all modifications"; consonant-to-vowel intensity ratio did not follow earlier findings).

**Listener side: what makes speech sound unclear.** [hazan2004] A: 45 talkers; intelligibility "significantly correlated with the total energy in the 1- to 3-kHz region and word duration; these measures predicted 61% of the variability"; rated articulation, voice dynamics and general quality correlate with it. [bradlow1996] F (abstract): 20 talkers x 100 sentences; "talkers with larger vowel spaces were generally more intelligible"; "F0 mean and speaking rate did not correlate with intelligibility"; listener errors of segment deletion "could be traced directly to detailed timing characteristics". [whitfield2017] A: rated clarity of healthy speakers follows working vowel space and articulation rate. [smiljanic2009]: "a direct relationship between acoustic-phonetic variation due to changes in speaking style and variability in intelligibility has been difficult to establish". [tu2016] A: recogniser performance on 32 dysarthric speakers is predicted by rated "articulatory precision contributing the most ... followed by prosody". No study of "mumbling" under that name was found.

**Methods I - posteriors of a recogniser against the known text.**
- *Goodness of pronunciation (GOP).* [cao2024] F (Sec. 2, S for Witt & Young 2000): the original score is the log posterior of the canonical phone over its aligned frames divided by their number; "A typical definition of DNN-based GOP is simply the average log-posterior". For CTC models: "only non-blank tokens contribute to the estimation, while the blank tokens are skipped" (GOP-CTC-align). Limits stated there: "mispronunciations can cause alignment errors" and the aligned score "does not account for deletion and insertion errors".
- *Alignment-free GOP.* [cao2024] Eq. 5: log of p(canonical transcription | audio) over p(any sequence in place of the target unit, same left and right context | audio); both "can be efficiently computed by the forward algorithm"; AUC on child speech, simulated errors: 0.824 (older DNN GOP), 0.988 (CTC-align), 0.989 / 0.986 (alignment-free); "accounting for deletion errors is important". [parikh2025phon] F: unrestricted variants "are computationally expensive and scale poorly". [parikh2025logit] F: softmax posteriors "suffer from overconfidence"; maximum logit agreed best with human ratings.
- *Articulatory precision.* [halpern2026pathbench] F (Sec. 3): "Articulatory Precision (AP) is the average posterior probability of aligned phonemes over active speech"; with the true text, ArtP reached mean r = 0.71 with rated intelligibility over six pathological datasets, equal to the best audio-reference method; without text, 0.66. [talkar2025] F (Eq. 1): mean over aligned frames of log P(expected phone) - log P(output phone); r = 0.77 with a clinical speech sub-score. Both use a phoneme model and report per speaker, not per word.
- *Recognition errors as intelligibility.* [gutz2022] A: 52 speakers with ALS, 20 controls; commercial recogniser word accuracy and human transcription "were not strongly correlated"; "ASR should not be used as a one-to-one proxy"; reliability was worst "for typical and mildly impaired severity groups"; mixing noise into the signal "to reduce a ceiling effect" helped. [halpern2026asric] F: word error rate between the greedy decode and a language-model decode needs no reference and tracks expert ratings.
- *Uncertainty of the recogniser.* [tu2022] F (Table 1, as extracted): negative entropy and confidence of a LibriSpeech-trained ensemble correlate 0.76-0.79 with listeners' word scores on noisy speech, with no intelligibility labels. The same score falls with noise, so alone it cannot tell articulation from channel - the project's own probes found the same.

**Methods II - distance to a reference rendition of the same words.**
- *P-STOI / P-ESTOI.* [janbakhshi2019] F (Sec. 2-4): one-third-octave band envelopes (15 bands) of the test utterance are aligned by DTW to a reference built from healthy speakers; per time frame "the linear correlation coefficient between J frequency bands is computed", averaged over 15 consecutive frames, then over the utterance. Correlation with rated intelligibility R = 0.95 (cerebral palsy, English) and 0.95 (ALS, French); "it is beneficial to use gender-specific reference representations". [janbakhshi2020] F: a text-to-speech reference performs "comparable to using natural speech references". No training. The per-frame intermediate value is local in time.
- *Neural acoustic distance.* [bartelds2022] F (Table 1, Sec. 4): DTW between wav2vec 2.0 hidden-layer frames of the same word from two speakers; r = -0.87 with human native-likeness ratings (fine-tuned English model, middle layer), against -0.71 for MFCCs and -0.34 for MFCCs on a second dataset; "minimally influenced by individual reference speaker differences"; unchanged when references were only men or only women; the MFCC version "was not invariant to characteristics of the recording device". In [halpern2026pathbench] this distance (layer 10 of wav2vec2-large) ties for best (r = 0.71). [mcintosh2026] F: the same idea with WavLM "exceeds human agreement on holistic and sentence-level phonetic scoring".
- *Comparison-based mispronunciation detection.* [lee_glass2012] F: DTW between a student and a teacher reading the same script, word-level features from "poorly matching alignment regions"; "Only reference speakers of the same gender as the student are used".

**Methods III - acoustic-phonetic measures.**
- *Vowel space area (VSA), formant centralisation ratio (FCR).* [sapir2010] F/A: FCR = (F2u + F2a + F1i + F1u) / (F2i + F1a); about 1.0 for normal men, women and children, expected "around 0.90" for clear speech, higher when centralised; built because "VSA is highly sensitive to interspeaker variability". VSA explains 6-46 % of variance in rated severity across studies [fletcher2017] F, 45 % in [turner1995] A, 6-8 % in [tjaden2004] via [lansford2014] F. Needs corner-vowel tokens and formant tracking.
- *Automatic working vowel space.* [sandoval2013] F: F1/F2 of all voiced frames (Praat LPC), outliers removed (about 15 %), k-means with 12 centres, convex-hull area; correlation 0.78 with hand-measured VSA on TIMIT using ten sentences per speaker. Trajectory-based measures did not predict intelligibility where the token-based VSA did (R2 = .26) [thompson2023] F; both kinds separate Parkinson's from controls [abur2026] F.
- *Long-term spectrum 1-3 kHz and envelope modulation.* Definitions in [krause2004]; [drullman1994] A: "at 8-10 Hz the temporal modulation spectrum is divided into two equally important parts", and reducing modulations below 4 Hz gives "no reduction of sentence intelligibility". Reverberation acts on the same quantities ([houtgast1985] A; [senoussaoui2015] F: the modulation-energy ratio has "high inter- and intra-speaker variability"). [liss2010] A: envelope modulation spectra need "no editing or linguistic assumptions".
- *Consonants.* Spectral moments every 10 ms through a burst or fricative classified stops at 92 % and sibilants at 98 % in read carrier phrases [forrest1988] A; moments and amplitude distinguish the four fricative places [jongman2000] A. Stop and flap weakening shows as a smaller amplitude dip [yun2020; warner2011]. A trained model of sonorant / continuant posteriors measures lenition [wayland2024] F.

**Fit.**
- Posterior scores: the per-word "compatibility" the project already stores is, by definition, GOP-CTC-align on characters. The earlier research rejected it as a clarity measure because noise, band-limiting and tempo lower it; the sources above agree on the confounds but also show that this is the standard articulation measure when the text is known. *Inference:* subtracting the participant's own clip median removes a stationary channel offset, and subtracting the reference reading's score for the same word removes word difficulty; neither normalisation is tested in the papers read (the nearest is the per-phoneme normalisation mentioned in [halpern2026pathbench]). Alignment-free GOP is computable from the stored emissions with two extra forward passes per word; on characters instead of phonemes it is untested.
- Greedy decode against transcript: available now; per [gutz2022] expect a ceiling for healthy readers - it will catch deletions and gross reductions (R3), not mild mumbling.
- Reference distance: one-third-octave envelopes are a cheap addition to the existing spectra, and the alignment replaces DTW at word level. wav2vec 2.0 hidden states come from the forward pass already made (the papers used larger models and middle layers; the base model is not evaluated there). The published versions average several references of the same sex; the project has one reference of another speaker.
- FCR / vowel space: needs a formant tracker and enough /i, u, a/ tokens; a 20-40 s passage may not contain them, and the reference text fixes which vowels occur. Not judged feasible per five-word window.
- Controlled edits that imitate the published patterns (none validated as such): move each frame's spectral envelope toward the clip's long-term mean envelope (vowel centralisation); attenuate bursts and frication in consonant spans from the alignment (weak obstruents, unreleased finals); cut the final consonant span (dropped ending); low-pass the band envelopes below about 8 Hz (reduced modulation depth, after [drullman1994]); compress vowel spans only (short vowels without the formant undershoot). The present spectral smoothing is closest to the first and the fourth.

### 2.5 What cannot be measured from an uploaded file

- Absolute loudness and audibility: the level norms ([rindel2019] table) refer to calibrated dB at 1 m; an upload has unknown gain and distance, and AGC [zhang_hansen2007]. Offered instead: level relative to the clip, and level-independent effort correlates (2.1). Absolute effort class: only with a trained classifier, 64-78 % on four classes.
- Projection to a room, listener distance, masking by the venue: properties of the transmission path ([houtgast1985]), absent from the file.
- Whether a quiet stretch was the speaker or the microphone: partly separable by spectral balance and F0 (2.1); not separable when the channel also filters.
- Nervousness as a state, and whether a rough voice is nervous, habitual or disordered: needs the speaker's own baseline; reviews find no consistent anxiety pattern (2.3).
- Intelligibility for human listeners: every method here is a proxy validated on pathological or second-language speakers, mostly per speaker; none is validated per five words on healthy read speech.

## 5. Open questions; searched for and not found

- No norm for intensity declination (dB/s) or for the size of the final drop in healthy read English; the only rate found is for source strength in a few Irish sentences. Searches: "intensity declination", "amplitude declination", "phrase-final intensity drop", Strik & Boves 1995 (not opened).
- No study that defines "uneven loudness" or "mumbling" as a rated flaw with acoustic correlates in healthy public speakers; clinical and clear-speech work is the nearest.
- No measurement of vocal tremor, breathiness or strain in nervous public speakers; the two public-speaking papers could not be opened.
- No lightweight blind detector of dynamic-range compression: [sun2026] F calls blind estimation "challenging" and the effect "often non-invertible", and solves it by optimisation. Whether a spectral-balance measure is unaffected by compression and AGC is untested.
- No validation of posterior scores, reference distances or vowel measures on windows of a few words, on healthy readers, or with a single reference of another speaker; no validation of character-level CTC posteriors as GOP.
- Zhang & Hansen's non-monotonic tilt table conflicts with the alpha and tilt results of other groups; which definition survives across microphones is open.
- The published rate "5 Hz/dB" for F0 comes from isolated vowels over three distances; its size in read sentences was not found.
- How much of a different-speaker posterior difference is accent rather than articulation is not addressed in the sources read.

**Where this report differs from the earlier research.** (1) `features/REPORT.md` sec. 4 calls the aligned posterior "confidence, not phonemic GOP" that "buys no validated construct": by the definitions in [cao2024] it is GOP-CTC-align, and [halpern2026pathbench], [talkar2025] validate the same average against intelligibility ratings (pathological speech, phoneme models). The confounds measured earlier stand. (2) The same report keeps alpha as a diagnostic "with no automatic penalty": [sundberg2006] and [eriksson2002] make spectral balance the main level-independent correlate of effort, i.e. the candidate for the effort flaw; its channel sensitivity is real and is why it is proposed only relative to the clip's own median. (3) `docs/PAPERS.md` items 19 and 44 (clear speech is a bundle) remain abstract-only here too; [smiljanic2009] is the full-text review that lists the bundle.
